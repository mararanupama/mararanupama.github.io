Marketing Campaign Performance Analysis

Using Power BI to understand customer response, evaluate contact efficiency and identify opportunities to improve a bank's term deposit campaigns.

Portfolio projectUCI Bank Marketing dataset

A Portuguese retail bank contacted clients to promote term deposits. I framed the work as a business intelligence problem: help campaign managers understand which segments and contact patterns were associated with stronger conversion, while keeping the limits of observational data visible.

The business questions

  • How many contacted clients subscribed, and what was the overall conversion rate?
  • Which customer, channel and campaign-history segments responded most strongly?
  • How did conversion change as repeated contact attempts increased?
  • Which findings could support practical campaign tests without being presented as causal proof?

Data preparation in Power Query

I separated the work into a source-preserving staging query and a cleaned campaign-contact query. The process removed 12 exact duplicate rows, assigned explicit data types, renamed fields in business language and retained Unknown as a visible source category rather than silently treating it as missing.

The source used pdays = 999 to mean that a client had not been contacted previously. I separated that code into a previous-contact flag and a nullable days-since-contact field. I also created numeric subscription flags and ordered age bands for reliable measures and visuals.

What the data showed

Overall conversion was 11.27%. Cellular contacts converted at 14.74%, compared with 5.23% for telephone contacts. A successful previous campaign outcome was the strongest observed segment, with a 65.11% conversion rate.

Contact efficiency weakened as attempts accumulated. Conversion declined from 13.04% on the first attempt to 5.49% for six or more attempts. Students and retired clients also showed high response rates, but their smaller volumes mean conversion and segment size should be considered together.

Recommendations

  • Prioritise structured follow-up for clients with a successful previous campaign outcome.
  • Test greater use of cellular contact where consent, regulation and operating practice allow it.
  • Review campaign effort after three attempts and introduce a stronger stop rule after five.
  • Investigate why May carried the highest contact volume but converted below the overall average.
  • Run controlled tests before treating any segment difference as a causal effect.

Important limitations

The dataset contains month and weekday labels but no complete contact date, so I did not manufacture a continuous time series. Call duration is only known after a call ends; it can explain completed-call outcomes but should not drive pre-contact targeting. The findings describe associations in historical campaign data and do not establish why clients subscribed.
